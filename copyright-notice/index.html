4. United States Copyright Notice

Under 17 U.S.C. §§ 101 and 103, a compilation may receive copyright protection where preexisting materials or data are selected, coordinated, or arranged in a sufficiently original manner. Copyright in a compilation extends only to the original material contributed by the compiler and does not create exclusive rights in preexisting public-domain material.

Accordingly, LWB asserts copyright in qualifying original selection, coordination, arrangement, editorial material, artwork, software, metadata, and other original expression contained in its products and Services. This claim is independent of the copyright or public-domain status of the underlying Scripture text.

No claim in mere typeface. United States copyright law does not recognize copyright in a typeface as such, and mere variations of typographic ornamentation or lettering are not independently copyrightable. LWB therefore does not base this Notice on ownership of ordinary typeface or mere typographic styling. LWB instead relies on its copyrightable original expression, compilation authorship, code, graphics, editorial works, and protectable edition or page-design elements.

Unauthorized reproduction, distribution, public display, republication, adaptation, or copying of LWB Protected Elements may infringe copyright even where the underlying Scripture itself is public domain.

5. European Union & United Kingdom Database / Edition Rights

In the European Union, qualifying databases may receive copyright protection in an original selection or arrangement and may also qualify for the separate sui generis database right under Directive 96/9/EC where the statutory requirements—including the required substantial investment in obtaining, verifying, or presenting database contents—are satisfied.

Where an LWB database qualifies for such protection, unauthorized extraction or reutilization of all or a substantial part of its protected contents may infringe applicable database rights. Nothing in this Notice claims a database right where the statutory requirements are not met.

In the United Kingdom, qualifying database rights are governed by the Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997, as amended. UK law also separately recognizes copyright in the typographical arrangement of a published edition under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988. That published-edition right has its own statutory scope and term and exists independently from copyright in the underlying literary work.

These rights, where applicable, protect LWB’s qualifying database or edition contributions and do not convert public-domain Scripture into proprietary text.
