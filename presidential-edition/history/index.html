The American Presidency

The Constitution established the executive branch and the office of President. George Washington took the first presidential oath of office on April 30, 1789. The office has since become the center of the federal executive branch and a defining institution in American civic history.

Did You Know? — How to address a President
According to the U.S. Department of State’s protocol guidance, correspondence to the sitting President is addressed to The President at the White House, with the salutation Dear Mr. President or Dear Madam President. The courtesy title The Honorable is used in writing for elected officials and is generally retained after leaving office.

Did You Know? — Formal correspondence
The Honorable William J. Clinton. U.S. protocol uses The Honorable as a written courtesy title for elected officials and generally retains it after they leave office. For the sitting President, formal White House correspondence is addressed to “The President.”
