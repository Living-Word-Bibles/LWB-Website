Living Word Bibles Editorial Standards and Practices

Living Word Bibles publishes and distributes Scripture and Bible-related material in digital and physical formats. These standards govern how we prepare, format, describe, review, correct, and present Bible texts and related editorial content across our website, online readers, downloadable eBibles, print editions, mobile and web applications, product pages, historical resources, maps, reference material, newsletters, and other Living Word Bibles publications.

Living Word Bibles is operated by Cook Services Company, LLC. Our publishing work is guided by a Christian mission, but editorial integrity requires that Scripture text, historical facts, source information, copyright status, translation labels, and the views of differing Christian traditions be represented accurately rather than altered to fit a marketing objective or a preferred conclusion.

We do not silently rewrite Scripture. We do not disguise commentary as Scripture. We do not knowingly publish a fabricated source, quotation, citation, historical claim, or textual reading.

1. Mission Statement

Beautifully Formatted to Bring God’s Word to Life on Any Device. At Living Word Bibles, our mission is simple: make God’s Word accessible to all.

That mission requires more than attractive design. It requires faithful Scripture text, readable typography, responsible publishing, accurate supporting material, accessible digital experiences, reliable product descriptions, transparent rights information, and a correction process that treats readers with respect.

2. Products and Formats Covered by These Standards

These standards apply to Living Word Bibles products and public-facing content regardless of the device, storefront, file type, or distribution channel through which a reader receives them.

Scripture Editions

Print Bibles, eBibles, PDFs, EPUB-style files, downloadable editions, study and reading editions, public-domain texts, and licensed translations.

Digital Experiences

Online Bible readers, web applications, mobile applications, search, chapter and verse navigation, bookmarks, highlights, notes, and related software features.

Website Content

Translation histories, Bible history, book introductions, canon resources, articles, FAQs, product descriptions, church resources, and educational pages.

Reference and Visual Material

Maps, timelines, charts, tables, photographs, manuscript images, illustrations, bibliographies, footnotes, cross-references, and metadata.

Audio Bible

Narrated Scripture, chapter audio, source recordings, playback interfaces, visual accompaniment, attribution, and audio metadata.

Accounts & Digital Libraries

Customer libraries, digital purchases and downloads, free Bible access, purchase matching, and account corrections.

3. Core Editorial Principles

Accuracy

We verify names, dates, titles, Scripture references, book names, chapter and verse numbers, historical claims, quotations, source attributions, product specifications, and rights notices before publication when reasonably possible.

Fairness

When describing differences among Christian traditions, canons, translations, textual traditions, denominations, or scholarly interpretations, we aim to describe each position in recognizable terms and avoid caricature or unnecessary polemic.

Transparency

Readers should be able to tell what is Scripture, what is a translator or publisher note, what is Living Word Bibles editorial material, what is a quotation from another source, and what is an interpretive or historical explanation.

Accountability

Material errors should be corrected. Where an error affects more than one format or edition, we review the related website page, download, app data, print master, metadata, and product listing so the correction is not confined to only one surface.

Faithful Stewardship

We treat the Bible as sacred Scripture and also as a text with identifiable editions, translations, manuscript traditions, publishers, rights holders, and bibliographic histories. Reverence is not a substitute for textual care; it is a reason to exercise it.

4. Scripture Text Fidelity

The biblical text is the foundation of every Living Word Bibles Scripture product. A translation or edition must not be silently paraphrased, modernized, harmonized, doctrinally revised, shortened, expanded, or blended with another version while continuing to carry the original version label.

  • Scripture text should be sourced from an identifiable edition, authorized digital source, publisher file, reliable public-domain transcription, or other documented source appropriate to the translation.
  • Spelling, punctuation, capitalization, paragraphing, poetic lineation, red-letter treatment, divine-name conventions, italics, small caps, footnote markers, and other textual features should follow the chosen source edition unless an editorial policy clearly identifies a formatting change.
  • Chapter and verse boundaries must be checked for omissions, duplication, transposition, broken anchors, accidental merges, and missing text.
  • Formatting changes may improve readability, responsive display, navigation, or typography, but they must not change the meaning of the underlying Scripture text.
  • If Living Word Bibles discovers that a source file itself contains a material textual error, the correction should be verified against an authoritative edition or rights-holder source before release.

No editorial preference, doctrinal dispute, software limitation, marketing objective, or design convenience justifies silently changing the words of a labeled Bible translation.

5. Translation and Edition Identification

Every Bible product should identify the translation or version clearly enough that a reader can understand what text is being read. Where appropriate, an edition should also identify language, revision, publication history, source edition, copyright or public-domain status, and publisher or rights-holder attribution.

Each Bible edition is prepared according to its own text, canon, tradition, and publishing requirements. We preserve those characteristics so readers receive the Bible they selected, clearly identified and faithfully presented.

When more than one translation appears on the website, each one should be clearly labeled. Wherever readers enter the site—from search results, shared links, or the Bible reader—the translation should remain easy to identify.

Editorial Standards for Presidential eBibles

Living Word Bibles Presidential eBibles are custom presentation editions that combine faithful Scripture with carefully selected presidential, historical, commemorative, and presentation material.

  • Every Presidential eBible clearly identifies its underlying Bible translation or edition.
  • Scripture remains the central text of the publication and is not rewritten or altered to reflect the political views, administration, party, policies, or personality of any President.
  • Presidential editions may include forewords, dedications, portraits, signatures, correspondence, speeches, proclamations, photographs, historical documents, timelines, prayers, and other appropriate supplementary material.
  • Added presidential and historical material remains clearly distinguishable from Scripture.
  • Names, dates, presidential titles, terms of office, quotations, speeches, correspondence, and historical descriptions should be presented accurately.
  • Historical speeches and public documents should be reproduced faithfully and in proper historical context.
  • Custom editions may document their presentation history, including when an edition was prepared, presented, sent, received, acknowledged, or used, and does not necessarily constitute an endorsement by the recipient or office to which it was presented.
  • Presidential imagery and historical material should complement the commemorative, historical, educational, and presentation character of the edition while preserving the integrity of the biblical text.
  • Each Presidential eBible follows the same Living Word Bibles standards for Scripture fidelity, translation identification, rights and attribution, digital quality, accessibility, historical accuracy, and corrections that apply throughout the Living Word Bibles catalog.
  • These presentation editions are prepared by Living Word Bibles for various Presidents and other heads of state around the world, and do not necessarily constitute an official endorsement by any specific head of state or government.

Presidential Editions preserve the integrity of Scripture while presenting it within a carefully prepared historical and commemorative edition honoring the office, history, and recipient for whom the edition was created.

Editorial Standards for Heads of State eBibles

Living Word Bibles Heads of State eBibles are individually prepared presentation editions created for heads of state, heads of government, governors-general, governors, prime ministers, monarchs, and other distinguished public offices and recipients.

  • Every Heads of State eBible clearly identifies the Bible translation, language, canon, or edition upon which it is based.
  • Scripture is preserved faithfully and is not rewritten to reflect the politics, policies, government, nationality, religious affiliation, or personal views of the recipient.
  • Custom material may include presentation pages, dedications, prayers, correspondence, portraits, signatures, biographies, speeches, photographs, historical documents, national material, and other appropriate supplementary content.
  • Supplementary material remains clearly distinguishable from Scripture.
  • Names, titles, honorifics, offices, dates of service, constitutional roles, and forms of address should be presented accurately and respectfully.
  • Living Word Bibles should accurately distinguish between a head of state and a head of government where those are separate constitutional offices.
  • Multilingual editions should use careful translations and appropriate forms of address while preserving the identity and meaning of the underlying Scripture edition.
  • Custom editions may document their presentation history, including when an edition was prepared, presented, sent, received, acknowledged, or used, and does not necessarily constitute an endorsement by the recipient or office to which it was presented.
  • National, royal, governmental, historical, and cultural material may be included when appropriate to the recipient and character of the edition.
  • Each Heads of State eBible follows Living Word Bibles standards for Scripture fidelity, translation identification, historical accuracy, rights and attribution, accessibility, digital quality, and corrections.
  • These presentation editions are prepared by Living Word Bibles for various heads of state around the world, and do not necessarily constitute an official endorsement by any specific head of state or government.

Heads of State Editions combine faithful Scripture with carefully prepared historical, cultural, and presentation material appropriate to the recipient, nation, and office represented.

6. Canon, Book Order, and Deuterocanonical Material

Christian traditions do not all use the same table of contents. We preserve the canon and book order of each edition and identify those differences clearly for readers.

  • The books included in an edition should match the edition’s stated canon or source tradition.
  • When an edition includes deuterocanonical, apocryphal, Orthodox, or Ethiopian books, their presence should be clear in the table of contents and product description.
  • Historical or educational pages discussing canon should distinguish between the practices of different churches and historical periods.
  • Alternative book names, numbering traditions, and book divisions should be explained where they could confuse readers.
  • Living Word Bibles should not imply that a single modern arrangement represents every Christian tradition.

Ethiopian Bible resources and product labeling

Living Word Bibles editorial and product material concerning the Ethiopian biblical tradition should distinguish between the broader Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canonical tradition and the contents of a specific Living Word Bibles product or PDF. A collection marketed as The Complete Apocrypha of the Ethiopian Bible should identify the books actually included and should not be described as the entire Ethiopian Bible or the complete Ethiopian canon unless the contents of that specific edition support that statement.

Book names, alternate titles, ordering, canonical status, manuscript or translation provenance, and explanatory claims should be checked carefully because Ethiopian, Orthodox, Catholic, and Protestant terminology may overlap without being identical.

7. Original Languages, Transliteration, and Ancient Texts

When Living Word Bibles publishes Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, Latin, Ge’ez, or other ancient-language material, the original script should be preserved where technically practical and reviewed for encoding, directionality, diacritics, punctuation, and font support.

Transliteration should use a consistent system within a page or publication. When a scholarly transliteration differs from a familiar English form, the page may provide both. Editorial material should distinguish a translation of an ancient-language term from a transliteration of that term.

Claims about manuscript readings, textual variants, the Masoretic Text, Septuagint, Vulgate, Textus Receptus, critical Greek texts, Dead Sea Scrolls, or other witnesses should identify the relevant source or scholarly basis when the distinction matters to the claim.

8. Scripture Rights, Copyright, and Publisher Licensing

Rights vary by translation, edition, territory, format, and rights holder. Some Scripture texts may be in the public domain in a particular jurisdiction; others may be protected by copyright, trademark, contractual license, Crown privilege, or publisher-specific quotation and distribution rules.

  • Before publishing or distributing a translation, Living Word Bibles should document the legal or contractual basis for the intended use.
  • Required copyright notices, trademarks, publisher credits, quotation notices, territorial restrictions, and attribution language must be preserved.
  • We do not claim ownership of underlying public-domain Scripture merely because it appears in a Living Word Bibles design or edition.
  • Living Word Bibles may separately own or license its formatting, typography, artwork, metadata, product descriptions, software, editorial notes, and compilation elements.
  • Rights questions and territorial restrictions are governed by the applicable translation license and our Copyright Notice and Publisher Licensing Notice.

For application and developer use, additional requirements may appear on our App Licensing page. This Editorial Standards page does not expand any license granted by a Bible publisher or rights holder.

Embed code and church/nonprofit translation use

The standard Living Word Bibles embed-code license is for the King James Version (KJV) only. An embed, widget, configuration option, technical capability, or appearance within Living Word Bibles software does not by itself grant a user the right to reproduce, distribute, sublicense, or publicly display another Bible translation.

Other translations may be made available for use by churches and religious nonprofit organizations only when that use is permitted by the applicable copyright holder, publisher, license, permission, or other controlling rights instrument. Any attribution, quotation limit, territorial restriction, noncommercial limitation, platform restriction, or other condition imposed by the rights holder remains controlling. Living Word Bibles does not sublicense rights it does not own.

9. Print Bible Production Standards

Print production introduces risks that do not exist in a browser: page imposition, trim, binding, gutter loss, font substitution, missing signatures, low-resolution artwork, and manufacturing defects can all interfere with the reading experience. Print masters should therefore be reviewed separately from digital files.

Pre-publication review

  • Confirm the edition title, translation label, front matter, copyright notice, table of contents, book order, chapter sequence, and closing matter.
  • Check verse continuity at page breaks, column breaks, section breaks, and book transitions.
  • Review poetry, paragraphing, headings, footnotes, cross-references, page headers, folios, and typography for consistency.
  • Check fonts, embedded characters, ligatures, diacritics, line spacing, margins, gutters, trim, bleed, cover dimensions, spine text, and barcode or ISBN metadata when applicable.
  • Review a print proof or representative production proof before a major release when reasonably practical.

A manufacturing defect should not be confused with a textual correction. If a print run has a material production defect, the product listing and support process should address it honestly.

10. eBibles, PDFs, EPUB-Style Files, and Digital Downloads

Digital editions must preserve the same Scripture fidelity as print and be easy to use. A file is not ready for publication if verses are missing, the table of contents is broken, footnotes lead to the wrong place, or the reading order does not work properly.

  • We test digital files to make sure they open properly, chapters and links work, the reading order is correct, search behaves as expected, and the edition displays well on common devices.
  • Book and chapter navigation should be built from verified Bible data so every link leads to the correct passage.
  • Footnotes and cross-references should lead readers to the correct passage and remain clearly separate from the biblical text.
  • Download names, edition information, file descriptions, and product pages should clearly identify the correct translation and format.
  • When we replace a file because of a meaningful correction, we update its version or date so readers and support staff can distinguish the corrected edition from an earlier copy.

Digital Account Libraries

When a Bible or other digital product is added to a reader's library, the title, translation, format, and download should match the product purchased or provided. Library access does not change the copyright or licensing status of the Scripture or other material in that edition.

  • The Holy Bible: King James Version Special Edition and the Douay-Rheims Bible may be provided free to eligible Living Word Bibles accounts.
  • Verified eBible purchases and qualifying digital PDFs, including the Living Word Bibles Ethiopian Apocrypha PDF where applicable, may be added to the purchaser’s library.
  • A print purchase does not include a digital copy unless the product description expressly says that it does.
  • When we match a past purchase to an account, we verify the transaction and add the correct product to the correct reader’s library.
  • Library access may be added, restored, removed, or corrected when needed for customer support, refunds, payment reversals, or accurate recordkeeping.

11. Website and Online Bible Reader Standards

Our online Bible reader is part of our publishing work, so the same standards of accuracy and Scripture fidelity apply online as they do to our downloadable and print editions. The passage shown must always match the translation, book, chapter, and verse selected by the reader.

  • Switching translations must never mix text from different Bible versions.
  • Links to a specific passage should open the intended Scripture and keep the correct translation selected whenever possible.
  • Book lists, chapter selectors, verse links, search results, and next/previous controls should all lead readers where they expect to go.
  • Highlights, notes, bookmarks, copy tools, and sharing features should remain visually distinct from Scripture.
  • If part of the reader is temporarily unavailable, we should not silently show the wrong translation or outdated editorial material in its place.

12. Apps, Software, and Interactive Bible Products

Our apps follow the same Scripture standards as our website and downloadable editions. Features designed for convenience must never quietly change the underlying biblical text.

Before release, we test the main reading experience, translation selection, book and chapter navigation, search, offline reading where offered, text sizing, screen orientation, accessibility labels, legal links, and the reader’s saved place.

If an app uses a third-party Scripture source, the translation name, attribution, license information, and edition shown to readers must match the text actually being delivered. If that source becomes unavailable, we will not substitute another translation under the same name.

13. Accessibility and Device Compatibility

Making Scripture accessible includes readers with visual, motor, auditory, cognitive, and other disabilities. Living Word Bibles aims to improve accessibility across its website and digital products using recognized web-accessibility practices, including the principles reflected in the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).

  • Use semantic headings, landmarks, lists, tables, labels, and reading order.
  • Provide useful alternative text for meaningful images while avoiding redundant descriptions for decorative graphics.
  • Support keyboard navigation and visible focus states for interactive web content.
  • Maintain readable contrast and permit text enlargement without forcing unnecessary loss of content or function.
  • Use descriptive link text and accessible labels for Bible-navigation controls.
  • Avoid embedding essential Scripture content only inside an image when text can be provided accessibly.

Accessibility is an ongoing quality process. We should not claim a formal accessibility certification or conformance level unless the relevant product has actually been evaluated against that standard.

14. Headings, Notes, Footnotes, Cross-References, and Added Editorial Material

Material added by Living Word Bibles must be clearly distinguishable from Scripture in both design and presentation. This includes headings that are not part of the source translation, introductions, study notes, explanatory notes, maps, captions, timelines, reading prompts, devotionals, cross-references, and other editorial features.

Where a heading or note could be mistaken for inspired biblical text, the design should make the distinction clear. Editorial notes should not be inserted into a verse as though they were part of the translation.

Cross-references are aids, not declarations that two passages have an identical meaning. They should be checked for valid destinations and presented as references rather than additions to the verse.

15. Bible History, Translation History, and Scholarly Content

Historical and educational pages should be researched with the same care applied to Scripture production. Dates, manuscript names, translation histories, councils, printers, publishers, archaeological discoveries, historical figures, and claims about the development of the biblical canon should be checked against reliable sources.

Where the historical record is disputed or scholarly conclusions remain provisional, Living Word Bibles should identify the uncertainty rather than present one reconstruction as undisputed fact. Traditional Christian claims may be described as tradition or confession when they cannot be established solely as historical fact.

Substantial historical pages should include a bibliography, source list, inline citations, or other reasonable means for readers to understand the basis of major claims.

16. Maps, Timelines, Archaeology, and Geographic Claims

Biblical geography often involves ancient place names, uncertain identifications, changing political boundaries, and competing archaeological proposals. Maps and timelines should distinguish a confirmed location or date from an approximate, traditional, proposed, or disputed identification.

  • Modern borders should not be projected backward into antiquity without explanation.
  • A map legend or caption should identify when a route, boundary, or location is approximate or reconstructed.
  • Artifact and manuscript images should include provenance or source credit when known and lawful to publish.
  • Illustrative reconstructions should not be captioned as documentary photographs of the event or object itself.

17. Theology, Denominations, and Christian Traditions

Living Word Bibles is a Christian Scripture publisher. At the same time, our Bible resources serve readers from many churches and traditions. When we explain a doctrinal issue, liturgical practice, canon, translation philosophy, or historical controversy, we aim to distinguish the position of a particular tradition from a claim shared across all Christianity.

Editorial content may reflect a Christian perspective, but it should not misquote Scripture, invent consensus, or falsely attribute a teaching to a church, council, scholar, translator, or denomination. Where an article presents interpretation or devotional application, that material should be identified as commentary or explanation rather than Scripture text.

18. Scripture Citations, Quotations, Sources, and Attribution

Scripture references should identify book, chapter, and verse clearly and use a consistent style within a page or publication. When more than one translation appears in the same article, the version should be identified where needed to prevent ambiguity.

For scholarship and editorial writing, Living Word Bibles may use recognized biblical-studies conventions such as those reflected in the SBL Handbook of Style, while adapting presentation for ordinary readers.

  • Direct quotations should reproduce the source accurately and use quotation marks or block quotation formatting as appropriate.
  • Paraphrases should not be presented as direct quotations.
  • Source titles, authors, translators, editors, dates, publishers, and URLs should be recorded accurately where cited.
  • Third-party research, images, and explanatory material should receive attribution when required by law, license, scholarly practice, or editorial fairness.

19. Images, Illustrations, Manuscripts, and Media

Images should be lawful to use, relevant to the page, and credited where required. A photograph of a manuscript, Bible, archaeological site, artwork, map, or historical figure should not be described in a way that overstates what the image proves.

Generated or heavily altered imagery may be used for decorative banners, conceptual illustrations, product mockups, or explanatory graphics, but it should not be represented as a photograph of a real manuscript, artifact, archaeological discovery, historical event, or person when it is not.

Captions should identify whether an image is documentary, archival, illustrative, reconstructed, generated, or a product rendering whenever that distinction is material to a reader’s understanding.

Audio Bible and narration standards

Audio Scripture should be identified by translation, book, chapter, source recording, and narrator or project attribution where reasonably available. The audio presented to a listener should correspond to the Scripture passage and translation identified by the player.

  • When Living Word Bibles uses a LibriVox recording, the source project and recording should be identified or credited in a reasonable manner even though LibriVox states that its recordings are placed in the public domain in the United States.
  • Living Word Bibles should distinguish the underlying narration from LWB’s player, navigation, metadata, artwork, slideshow, chapter mapping, and other added presentation elements.
  • Images displayed during Bible audio should follow the same sourcing, attribution, historical-accuracy, and generated-image rules that apply elsewhere on the site.
  • If verse-level timing, image timing, chapter synchronization, or other audio alignment is approximate rather than exact, the interface or accompanying explanation should not represent it as exact.
  • Editing, normalization, compression, silence removal, or technical processing should not materially change the spoken Scripture or create a misleading impression about the identity of the narrator or source recording.

20. Technology-Assisted and AI-Assisted Publishing Workflows

Living Word Bibles may use software, automation, AI, and other digital tools to help with formatting, file conversion, proofreading, artwork, research, accessibility, link checking, search, and draft preparation.

Technology can assist our work, but people remain responsible for the Scripture and editorial material we publish.

  • AI and automated tools must not rewrite a Bible translation and present the result as the original translation.
  • Historical claims, quotations, citations, manuscript descriptions, copyright information, and bibliographic details produced with technological assistance must be checked before publication.
  • When we convert an edition from one format to another, we check for missing verses, damaged characters, broken footnotes, lost formatting, poetry errors, and incorrect book order.
  • Generated images must never be presented as documentary evidence of a real manuscript, object, person, or historical event.
  • Living Word Bibles remains responsible for the material we publish.

21. Metadata, Search, Structured Data, and SEO

Titles, descriptions, search information, and other metadata should accurately describe what readers will actually receive. We do not mislabel a translation, canon, product type, language, price, availability, or edition simply to attract attention.

Book, chapter, verse, translation, language, title, web address, social-preview image, ISBN or product identifier, file version, and publication date should agree with the edition being presented.

When Scripture data moves between publishing systems, we may use established standards such as the Open Scriptural Information Standard (OSIS) to help preserve book, chapter, verse, and other structural information accurately.

22. Corrections, Clarifications, and Version Control

When we confirm a meaningful error, we correct it. The way we make that correction depends on whether the issue affects Scripture, editorial material, a digital feature, publication information, or a physical product.

Scripture corrections

A suspected Scripture-text error should be checked against the documented source edition or rights-holder source before correction. We should not “correct” an unfamiliar reading merely because another translation uses different wording.

Editorial corrections

Errors in history pages, biographies, maps, bibliographies, captions, dates, product descriptions, or explanatory material should be corrected promptly once confirmed. Material corrections may include an update or correction note when useful to readers.

Corrections Across Formats

When the same material appears in more than one place, a meaningful correction should be carried through to the website, downloadable edition, app, print master, search information, social previews, and product listing where applicable.

Account and Library Corrections

We may correct a reader’s account or digital library when purchase, refund, payment, or support records show that access should be added, restored, removed, or moved to the correct account. These account corrections never change the Scripture or editorial content of the product itself.

Minor typography, spacing, broken-link, or layout fixes that do not change meaning may be corrected without a formal correction notice.

23. Quality Assurance and Release Review

Before a major Bible edition or digital release is published, we review it according to its size, format, and complexity.

  • Confirm the translation, source, rights notices, and front matter.
  • Check that books, chapters, and verses are complete, using automated comparison where useful and human review throughout the process.
  • Pay special attention to poetry, footnotes, red-letter passages, tables, special characters, deuterocanonical sections, long genealogies, and transitions between books.
  • Check navigation, internal links, downloads, images, accessibility labels, and responsive layouts.
  • Test web products on representative desktop, tablet, and mobile devices.
  • Record the release date or edition version when future corrections or updates are likely.

24. Commerce, Donations, Affiliates, and Editorial Independence

Sales, donations, advertising, and business relationships do not change our editorial standards. Scripture, historical material, and product information must remain accurate regardless of how a product is funded, promoted, or distributed.

Product descriptions should tell readers what they are receiving, including the format, translation, included material, compatibility, and any important restrictions. Promotional language may describe an edition’s design and features, but it must remain accurate.

Donors, advertisers, marketplaces, payment processors, and technology providers do not determine the wording of Scripture or the factual content of Living Word Bibles editorial material.

25. Reader Feedback, Error Reports, and Submissions

Readers are encouraged to report suspected textual errors, broken links, incorrect historical claims, accessibility problems, formatting defects, or mislabeled products. A useful report should include the page or product, translation, book/chapter/verse if applicable, device or file format, and the specific issue observed.

We review good-faith correction requests on their merits. A disagreement with a translation choice, canonical tradition, or scholarly conclusion does not automatically establish an error, but it may warrant clarification or additional sourcing.

26. Third-Party Publishers, Platforms, Retailers, and Services

Living Word Bibles works with outside publishers, rights holders, libraries, archives, retailers, app stores, payment services, print providers, and other platforms that help us publish and distribute our work.

We identify and credit outside publishers, services, and sources when they contribute to our products or help readers understand where material comes from. Mentioning or linking to an outside organization does not by itself mean that organization endorses Living Word Bibles or that Living Word Bibles endorses it.

When an outside platform controls payment, delivery, app review, or technical availability, we distinguish those platform decisions from our editorial decisions about Scripture and published content.

Public-domain audio and LibriVox

When LibriVox material is used, Living Word Bibles should preserve accurate source information and, where practical, identify the LibriVox project or reader associated with the recording. LibriVox states that its texts and recordings are public domain in the United States, while also cautioning that copyright status can differ outside the United States. Living Word Bibles should therefore avoid implying that a U.S. public-domain determination automatically resolves copyright status in every jurisdiction.
