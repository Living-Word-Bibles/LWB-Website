Editorial Standards

Our standards for Scripture integrity, translation licensing, historical and study content, audio, Ethiopian Bible resources, digital products, corrections, AI-assisted work, and the separation of editorial and commercial functions.

Effective: 30 September 2025Last Updated: 1 September 2026Operated by Cook Services Company, LLC
Scripture first. LWB distinguishes biblical text from commentary, preserves required translation notices, corrects material errors when identified, and does not treat editorial convenience as permission to alter Scripture.

1. Mission & Scripture First

Living Word Bibles exists to make Scripture readable, accessible, and beautifully presented across devices while respecting the text, its history, the rights of translators and publishers, and the diversity of Christian traditions.

Editorial commentary, history, marketing copy, and technical interface text must remain distinguishable from Scripture itself.

2. Scripture Text Integrity

Where LWB presents a named Bible translation, the goal is to reproduce the authorized or public-domain source text faithfully. Formatting may adapt to responsive screens, accessibility, navigation, verse display, and product layout, but such formatting should not silently rewrite a translation.

Book names, chapter mapping, verse numbers, punctuation, footnotes, headings, and textual variants are handled according to the source edition and the limits of the applicable license.

3. Sources, Citations & Scholarship

Historical, bibliographic, and educational pages should rely on responsible sources appropriate to the subject. LWB should distinguish primary sources, scholarly interpretation, denominational or confessional viewpoints, public-domain historical material, and editorial synthesis.

Where reasonable, substantial historical or study articles should identify sources or provide bibliographies so readers can verify important claims.

4. Translation & Licensing Integrity

LWB maintains translation-specific copyright, trademark, and attribution information. Public-domain status, open-license status, and publisher permission are not interchangeable. A translation's controlling rightsholder terms take priority over a general site summary.

The standard public embed license is KJV-only. Other translations may be considered only for churches and religious nonprofits within the applicable rightsholder's terms. See App Licensing and Publisher Licensing.

5. Audio Bible Standards

Audio Bible pages should identify the Scripture translation, the audio source or narrator where known, and the legal status or source license applicable to the recording. Chapter and verse navigation should be mapped carefully and any known approximation in timing or verse-level positioning should be disclosed rather than presented as exact.

Public-domain Bible imagery used in an audio slideshow should retain source or credit information when available. LWB-created player interfaces, metadata, curation, and visual arrangements should not be confused with ownership of a third-party or public-domain recording.

6. Ethiopian Bible Standards

LWB should clearly distinguish the historic Ethiopian Orthodox biblical tradition, scholarly discussion of the broader canon, and the contents of a particular LWB product. The product titled The Complete Apocrypha of the Ethiopian Bible is a specific digital collection and should not be marketed as though that PDF alone is identical to every formal enumeration of the Ethiopian Orthodox canon.

Descriptions should identify the books actually included, preserve historical nuance, and avoid claiming exclusive ownership over ancient or public-domain source texts. LWB may claim only its protectable edition, compilation, formatting, metadata, cover, and original editorial contributions.

7. Digital Products & Account Entitlements

Product titles, formats, download files, prices, product descriptions, and account entitlements should correspond to the product actually delivered. A PDF should be identified as a PDF; an eBible/EPUB should be identified accordingly; print products should not be represented as automatically including a digital entitlement unless expressly offered.

Purchase reconciliation or administrative account corrections may repair access records, but those processes must not silently alter Scripture content or misstate what a customer purchased.

8. AI-Assisted Work

LWB may use software or AI-assisted tools for drafting, formatting, code assistance, research organization, metadata, image preparation, or quality review. Human editorial responsibility remains with LWB. AI-generated statements should not be treated as self-validating authority for theology, history, copyright status, legal rights, or Scripture text.

Material factual, legal, historical, licensing, or textual claims should be checked against appropriate sources before publication.

9. Corrections & Version Control

Material errors should be corrected when identified. Corrections involving Scripture text, publisher notices, product files, rights status, or account fulfillment receive heightened review because mistakes can affect readers, publishers, or customers.

Release notes and timestamps should accurately describe significant site and policy changes without implying that every editorial correction creates a new legal right.

10. Advertising & Commercial Separation

Advertising, affiliate links, product merchandising, donations, and commercial calls to action should remain distinguishable from Scripture text and editorial analysis. Commercial relationships do not control LWB's editorial conclusions about Scripture, history, or translation status.

11. Privacy & Administrative Integrity

Editorial and administrative operations should avoid publishing private customer, subscriber, transaction, or account information. Site logging and portal audit records are operational records governed by the Privacy Policy and should not be repurposed as public editorial content without a lawful and appropriate basis.
